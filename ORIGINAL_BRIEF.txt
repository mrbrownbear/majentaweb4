```text
Create a premium, cinematic, architecture led landing page for a brand called "Majenta".

CORE EXPERIENCE

The entire website should be built around one continuous, highly detailed 3D architectural transformation.

The experience begins with an architectural blueprint.

As the user scrolls, the blueprint gradually becomes a real building.

The user should then travel through different architectural areas of that same building as they continue scrolling.

At the end of the journey, the building should transform, deconstruct, or recompose into the word:

MAJENTA

This must feel like one uninterrupted cinematic sequence rather than separate webpage sections.

The building is the website.

Do not make a normal website with a 3D building floating behind HTML content.

Build an authored architectural world where scrolling controls construction, camera position, lighting, materials, environment, typography, and spatial storytelling.

Use a detailed production quality 3D architectural model created specifically for this experience.

Do not use primitive boxes as the final building.

The building should contain a proper facade, glazing, structural framing, floors, stairs, corridors, internal spaces, architectural detailing, realistic materials, furniture where appropriate, lighting fixtures, landscaping, and enough internal geometry to allow the camera to physically travel through it.

Use Crea8 Worlds' full asset workflow.

First design concept imagery for the architectural asset.

Then generate the detailed 3D building through Hyper3D Rodin.

The resulting model must have sufficient geometry and material separation for individual areas and architectural components to animate during the scroll journey.

VISUAL DIRECTION

Style:
Contemporary luxury architecture
Editorial architectural photography
Premium property developer campaign
Minimal Swiss inspired graphic design
Architectural competition presentation
Cinematic architectural film

The overall look should feel sophisticated, precise and expensive.

Avoid a futuristic gaming aesthetic.

Avoid excessive neon.

Avoid generic glowing particles.

Avoid floating glass cards.

Avoid random blobs.

Avoid generic SaaS design.

Avoid decorative 3D elements that do not belong to the architecture.

The architecture itself should provide the spectacle.

COLOUR LANGUAGE

Use a controlled neutral architectural palette:

warm architectural white
soft concrete grey
charcoal
black
brushed aluminium
glass
muted stone
subtle timber accents

Introduce a distinctive Majenta accent colour derived from magenta, but use it very selectively.

The Majenta colour should appear primarily in:

technical blueprint markings
selected architectural details
navigation markers
light reflections
small typography details
the final MAJENTA word reveal

It should never overwhelm the architecture.

TYPOGRAPHY

Use elegant contemporary grotesk typography.

Typography should feel architectural and editorial.

Large headings can behave like spatial graphic elements.

Use oversized type sparingly.

Keep copy minimal.

Text should appear only when it contributes to the architectural journey.

Avoid conventional section cards.

Where possible, typography should appear integrated into the 3D environment through depth, masking, architectural alignment, clipping, projections, or spatial positioning.

SCROLL MECHANISM

Build the experience using:

Three.js or React Three Fiber
GSAP
ScrollTrigger
Lenis

Scrolling must control a deterministic reversible cinematic timeline.

Scrolling downward progresses construction and camera movement.

Scrolling upward perfectly reverses the entire journey.

No hard scene cuts unless deliberately motivated.

The camera should continuously move through the same architectural world.

Use approximately 800vh to 1200vh of scroll distance to give the architecture enough time to breathe.

STORYBOARD

ACT 01
THE DRAWING

The page begins almost completely white.

The building does not exist yet.

The camera looks down onto an architectural blueprint lying on a large drafting surface or appearing as a spatial architectural drawing.

Fine dark blue technical lines begin revealing themselves.

Show:

floor plans
structural grids
measurement annotations
elevation lines
construction marks
section lines
architectural notes
dimension arrows

Lines should animate as though a technical drawing is being plotted.

Use very subtle paper texture or drafting surface detail.

Small editorial typography appears:

MAJENTA

ARCHITECTURE IN MOTION

or similarly restrained copy.

The drawing should feel authentic, not decorative.

ACT 02
THE PLAN BECOMES SPACE

As scrolling continues, the 2D blueprint slowly gains depth.

Walls begin extruding vertically from the plan.

Columns emerge.

Structural beams assemble.

Floors gain thickness.

Staircases form.

The camera gradually changes from a top down orthographic feeling into a perspective architectural camera.

Blueprint lines remain visible temporarily around the growing building.

This transition should be extremely smooth.

The visitor should clearly understand:

drawing
becomes
structure.

ACT 03
STRUCTURAL ASSEMBLY

Continue constructing the building.

The camera slowly orbits while architectural systems assemble.

Reveal:

concrete slabs
steel framing
columns
cores
stairs
facade supports
roof geometry

Components can rise into place with restrained mechanical precision.

No explosive animations.

Movement should feel engineered.

Blueprint lines gradually fade as real materials appear.

The environment transitions from blueprint white into a softly lit architectural studio atmosphere.

ACT 04
THE FACADE

The camera reaches an exterior three quarter view.

The building is now nearly complete.

Glass facade systems slide into position.

Window panels install sequentially.

Metal frames lock into place.

Balconies and architectural fins appear.

Landscape elements emerge around the base.

Lighting becomes more cinematic.

Use early morning or late afternoon architectural light.

Introduce reflections across glass and brushed metal.

The building should now feel photorealistic and highly detailed.

This is the first major hero moment.

Use restrained text such as:

DESIGNED FROM FIRST PRINCIPLES

ACT 05
APPROACH

As the user scrolls, the camera begins moving toward the building.

The scale should become increasingly apparent.

Move past landscaping.

Pass facade details close to the camera.

Allow foreground architecture to briefly occlude the frame.

Create parallax between:

landscape
facade
structural elements
interior spaces.

The transition should feel like a Steadicam architectural film.

ACT 06
THE ENTRANCE

The camera approaches the main entrance.

Doors open or retract naturally.

The camera passes through the threshold.

Lighting transitions from exterior daylight into warmer architectural interior lighting.

Use reflections on polished stone or concrete.

Reveal a dramatic lobby.

Possible content:

MAJENTA
SPACES DESIGNED AROUND PEOPLE

Typography should feel embedded in the composition rather than sitting in a normal webpage container.

ACT 07
THE ATRIUM

Move through a large double height atrium.

This should be one of the strongest visual moments.

Features may include:

large staircase
architectural lighting
double height glazing
sculptural structural elements
vertical planting
suspended fixtures
mezzanine bridges

The camera can rise slightly as the visitor scrolls.

Allow light shafts to move subtly through the space.

People are optional and should only be used as subtle scale references.

ACT 08
MATERIAL DETAIL

Instead of immediately moving to another room, briefly slow the camera.

Move close to architectural details.

Reveal:

stone texture
timber grain
glass reflections
metal joints
lighting fixtures
fabric
concrete

This section should demonstrate craftsmanship.

A restrained label may appear:

MATERIAL
LIGHT
PROPORTION

No conventional feature cards.

ACT 09
THE STAIRCASE

Use the building's main staircase as a transition device.

The camera travels upward.

The user should feel spatial movement between floors.

As the camera climbs, architecture passes around the lens.

Use strong foreground framing.

The staircase can curve, cantilever, or form a dramatic central architectural gesture.

ACT 10
THE WORKSPACE

Enter another major area.

For example:

gallery
office
studio
residential lounge
creative workspace

depending on the architectural language selected for Majenta.

This area should feel distinct from the lobby.

Use large windows overlooking the exterior environment.

The camera travels through furniture and structural framing.

Copy can reveal itself using architectural masking.

Example:

SPACES THAT ADAPT

ACT 11
THE COURTYARD

Transition toward an internal courtyard or terrace.

Move from enclosed architecture into open air.

Introduce trees, planting, water or landscape elements.

The camera should briefly breathe here.

Slow the scroll response slightly.

Use subtle atmospheric movement:

leaves
soft reflections
moving sunlight
gentle shadow movement

This acts as a quiet moment in the experience.

ACT 12
THE SKY LEVEL

Continue upward.

The camera travels toward the upper levels.

Pass through another architectural corridor.

Move onto a rooftop terrace or high level viewing space.

Reveal the entire building and surrounding environment.

This should communicate scale.

The camera can orbit slowly around the upper facade.

ACT 13
ARCHITECTURAL EXPLODED VIEW

Now begin transitioning from physical architecture back toward architectural abstraction.

Pull the camera away.

Sections of the building subtly separate.

Floors move apart vertically.

Facade systems shift outward.

Structural components become visible.

Create an elegant exploded architectural diagram.

Do not destroy the building violently.

The movement should feel like a professional architectural visualization.

Technical lines and blueprint annotations can reappear.

ACT 14
DECONSTRUCTION

As the visitor approaches the final section, begin transforming the building into a graphic composition.

Architectural elements separate further.

Walls, slabs, facade panels, framing and structural lines begin aligning themselves into deliberate positions.

The building should gradually stop reading as architecture.

From the camera's changing angle, pieces begin forming letter shapes.

The transformation should happen progressively across several scroll beats.

ACT 15
MAJENTA

This is the final signature moment.

The camera arrives at one exact composition.

All architectural elements align to form:

MAJENTA

The letters should literally be constructed from geometry originating from the building.

Possible approach:

facade panels become letter surfaces
structural beams become strokes
glass elements become internal letter planes
blueprint lines complete missing letter contours
lighting reveals the final typography

Do not simply fade the building away and display HTML text.

The architecture itself must become the word.

The transition must be visually understandable.

The viewer should be able to watch pieces of the building physically becoming the letters.

Once assembled, hold this composition for a moment.

The environment simplifies.

Use a refined architectural white or very dark background depending on which provides stronger contrast.

The Majenta accent colour can finally become more prominent.

FINAL FOOTER

Immediately after the 3D MAJENTA transformation, transition into a minimal footer.

Keep it extremely clean.

Possible structure:

MAJENTA

Architecture
Development
Design

Selected links

Contact

Small copyright

The footer should feel like the calm conclusion of the architectural film.

CAMERA LANGUAGE

Treat every camera state like architectural cinematography.

Avoid random orbiting.

Use a carefully planned sequence of:

orthographic top view
perspective transition
three quarter exterior
facade approach
threshold crossing
interior dolly
vertical stair movement
corridor traversal
courtyard reveal
high level panorama
architectural pullback
exploded diagram view
final typography alignment

Use slow acceleration and deceleration.

Avoid motion sickness.

Camera paths must never clip through geometry accidentally.

Foreground architecture can intentionally cross the camera for cinematic transitions.

LIGHTING

Lighting should evolve with the story.

Blueprint phase:
very soft neutral studio lighting

Construction phase:
clean directional architectural light

Exterior:
warm late afternoon or early morning sunlight

Interior:
warm practical architectural lighting combined with daylight

Courtyard:
soft natural daylight

Final exploded sequence:
controlled studio lighting

Final Majenta logo:
dramatic but minimal sculptural illumination

Use PBR materials and physically plausible light relationships.

Use ACES tone mapping.

Use environment reflections carefully.

MATERIALS

Materials should be realistic and restrained.

Concrete:
subtle roughness and imperfection

Glass:
physically believable reflections and transmission

Metal:
brushed aluminium or dark anodized metal

Stone:
fine texture variation

Timber:
natural grain without excessive saturation

Blueprint:
thin emissive or graphic line treatment

Avoid overly glossy plastic surfaces.

INTERACTION DETAILS

Scrolling should remain the main interaction.

Optional subtle pointer response can affect:

small camera parallax
light reflections
architectural blueprint cursor
minor depth response

Do not allow pointer controls to interfere with the scroll camera.

Add a minimal scroll progress indicator inspired by architectural measurement markings.

For example:

01 DRAW
02 BUILD
03 ENTER
04 EXPLORE
05 ASCEND
06 TRANSFORM

This should remain visually quiet.

TRANSITIONS

Do not use basic fades between sections.

Use architectural transitions such as:

camera passing behind walls
doorway reveals
foreground occlusion
changes in focal length
material transitions
lighting transitions
blueprint lines becoming geometry
geometry becoming typography

Every transition should belong to the architectural concept.

Use:

compressed textures

instancing for repeated architecture
shared materials
adaptive DPR

frustum culling
reasonable shadow resolution

Desktop should retain the full experience.

Mobile should use a recomposed camera path with reduced decorative geometry while preserving the same architectural narrative.

Do not simply shrink the desktop composition.

REDUCED MOTION

Provide an elegant reduced motion mode.

Instead of removing the story entirely, use fewer interpolated camera movements and clearer stepped architectural states.

The final experience should resemble a premium architectural campaign created by a top international architecture visualization studio.

to
EXPLODED ARCHITECTURE

MAJENTA

The 3D building is the central asset and must be visually impressive enough to carry the entire experience.

Prioritise:

architectural model quality
camera choreography
materials
lighting
spatial composition
smooth reversible scrolling

over excessive special effects.

The final impression should be that the visitor did not scroll through a website.

They travelled through the creation of Majenta.
```
